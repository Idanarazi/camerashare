# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Two paired mobile users: one is the **Photographer** (holds the phone with the camera, receives real-time directions), one is the **Director** (watches the live feed on their own screen, sends directional cues). Used informally by couples, friends, or small content creator pairs. Neither user is a professional — they want a great shot without endless retakes. Both users are typically outdoors or in casual environments, phone in hand, physically separate by a few feet to many meters.

## Product Purpose

PicMe eliminates the "hand me the phone, no wait, tilt it a bit — no the other way" loop. The Photographer keeps their eye on the scene; the Director sees exactly what the camera sees and sends live commands (move, tilt, step back, capture). Success = fewer retakes, better shots, no frustration.

## Brand Personality

Cinematic · Decisive · Effortless. PicMe is a professional tool in consumer clothing. It doesn't try to be fun or social — it's a precision instrument for getting the shot. The interface should feel like a high-end camera app: dark, focused, purposeful.

## Anti-references

- **Generic SaaS (Notion/Linear)**: sidebar nav, white backgrounds, table-heavy dashboards. PicMe is a camera tool, not a productivity app.
- **AI-generated dark apps**: neon blue glows on everything, gradient cards floating in darkness, backdrop-blur on every surface, emoji used as icons. These patterns feel cheap and off-brand.
- **Consumer social (Instagram/TikTok)**: playful, colorful, story-shaped. PicMe is not entertainment.

## Design Principles

1. **Camera app, not chat app.** Every design decision should reference professional camera tools (ProCamera, Halide, native iOS Camera) not SaaS dashboards. The live viewfinder IS the product; the UI is a thin layer on top of it.
2. **Two jobs, one app.** Photographer and Director have completely different needs. The interfaces should feel subtly distinct — same DNA, different roles.
3. **Zero noise during the shot.** When the camera is live, controls must not compete with the image. Anything not essential should be hidden or whispered.
4. **Conventions earn trust.** Red shutter button. Green live indicator. Yellow warning. These are camera conventions. Don't subvert them without reason.
5. **Clarity over cleverness.** The QR code, room code, and knock flow must be obvious to a non-technical user seeing them for the first time.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG AA minimum. All interactive elements must meet 44×44px minimum touch target. Focus states must be visible. Animations must respect prefers-reduced-motion. Color is never the only signal.
